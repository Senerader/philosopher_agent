In this organization, engineers appear to orient primarily around their current
projects rather than around their functional software engineering team.

This is not only a motivation issue. It is an engineering-system friction point:
if people identify mainly with the project they are temporarily assigned to,
then the functional team can lose its role as the place where standards,
shared practices, peer support, and long-term identity are reinforced.

Within ESSP, this should be treated first as a step 1 diagnosis problem rather
than as a performance-management problem. The likely primary zone is developer
happiness, especially belonging, trust, and perceived connection to the team.
The likely companion dimension is communication and collaboration, because weak
team identity often reduces cross-project knowledge sharing and makes the team
feel like a staffing pool instead of a real unit.

The immediate implication is that the organization should not jump directly to
individual output targets. It should first baseline whether engineers feel that:

- they belong to a functional team, not only to a project
- the team has its own goals and identity
- cross-project collaboration inside the team is expected and useful
- team rituals help them do better work rather than add overhead

This working hypothesis should be validated with short surveys, manager and
engineer interviews, and a small set of team-level collaboration signals before
goals are finalized.
