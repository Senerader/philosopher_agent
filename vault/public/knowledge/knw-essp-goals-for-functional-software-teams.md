The ESSP adaptation for these functional software teams should use team
belonging as a primary improvement target, while keeping delivery metrics as
guardrails rather than as the main motivational lever.

The intended future state is:

- engineers feel that they belong to a functional software engineering team
- each team has visible goals beyond the projects currently assigned to it
- the team becomes a place for standards, peer support, and learning across
  projects
- stronger team identity improves delivery sustainability instead of competing
  with project execution

Recommended ESSP framing:

- Primary zone: developer happiness
- Companion dimension: communication and collaboration
- Guardrail zones: velocity and quality

Recommended team-level goals:

- establish a clear team identity and purpose for each functional team
- create a small set of team goals that are independent from any single project
- increase cross-project collaboration and knowledge sharing inside each team
- improve engineers' perception that team rituals are useful and connected to
  real work

Suggested leading indicators for the first rollout:

- a short belonging survey question such as "I feel part of my functional team"
- a short clarity question such as "I understand my team's goals beyond my
  current project"
- participation rate in team rituals that are meant to create team cohesion
- cross-project peer review or design review activity inside the same
  functional team
- number of knowledge-sharing sessions, reusable practices, or standards
  produced by the team

Suggested guardrail metrics from ESSP:

- flow state experience, to check that rituals and alignment work do not create
  extra fragmentation
- lead time, to confirm that stronger team identity is not slowing delivery
- change failure rate or another quality signal, to confirm that team alignment
  improves execution rather than merely adding coordination

Recommended rollout:

1. Run a 2-4 week baseline using a short survey and a few team-level signals.
2. Define 2-3 team goals per functional team that complement project goals.
3. Pilot rituals that reinforce team identity, such as team demos, shared
   retrospectives, technical standards reviews, or cross-project knowledge
   sharing.
4. Review early survey movement and collaboration signals before setting harder
   targets.
5. Only then set numeric goals, preferably as improvement from baseline rather
   than as arbitrary absolute benchmarks.

Recommended constraint:

Do not use these goals to rank individual engineers against one another. The
goals should strengthen the functional team as a unit. If the measurement
system becomes individual and competitive too early, it will likely worsen the
same belonging problem it is trying to solve.
