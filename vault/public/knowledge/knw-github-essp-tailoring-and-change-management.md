Source: `vault/public/assets/2025-05-28-GitHub-ESSP-Ebook-EZ-Version012.pdf`, pages 28-30.

GitHub explicitly says the playbook should be tailored to team context rather than applied as a universal recipe. Tailoring includes choosing which zones to emphasize, selecting telemetry versus surveys based on practical cost, and pairing downstream metrics with leading indicators that fit local friction points.

The document frames change management as part of engineering improvement, not as a separate afterthought. It points to ADKAR, Kotter, and Lewin as useful structures for helping teams understand why new metrics, tools, and practices are being introduced and how adoption should be supported.

This section also argues for a growth mindset around interventions: failed experiments still produce learning if teams inspect them honestly. Gamification is acceptable only when it reinforces intrinsically valuable behavior such as quality, collaboration, and team milestones. Metrics or rewards that push individuals toward quantity over quality are treated as a risk because they can distort the engineering system.
